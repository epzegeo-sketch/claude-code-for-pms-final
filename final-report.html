What happened

On 12 August the ping wait dropped from 90 to 60 seconds, and a scoring rule did the rest.

Release 4.2 changed three values: ping wait 90s → 60s, proximity weight 0.45 → 0.60, recent-acceptance weight 0.40 → 0.25. Two changes shipped together, so effects overlap, but the shorter wait is the main suspect for the misses.

Why it compounded

A missed ping was scored the same as a turn-down, and nothing ever gives points back.

↻ the loop closes on itself: starved responders can't recover.
Marcus's question answered

Weights are global. Nothing treats responders who turn pings down differently; it "fell out that way". The four weren't habitual turn-downs (21.6% vs 20.9% for everyone else).

03
Rewind
Finding: the four, and the tickets

Four responders (Farlight, Meteor Mite, The Undertow, Vesper) went from 11–15 pings a week to 1–5, nearly all missed. Their share of all pings fell from about 28% to about 9%. Every ticket claim I could check matched the ping counts, but only two of the four filed tickets at all.

The prompt that exposed it
Show me Vesper's full history of data.
The prompt that cross-checked it
Compare the support-tickets table on rook-database against the pings table. Do they agree with each other?
04
X-Ray Vision
Finding: the code scores a miss like a turn-down

A missed ping costs −0.12, the same as a turn-down. A taken ping adds only +0.08. There is no decay (a 2019 TODO never built), so a quiet responder can't climb back without being pinged. This also answered Marcus's 14 Aug question: the change applied to everyone.

The prompt that found it
Find the part of this code that takes points off somebody when they miss a ping or turn one down. Show it to me and explain it in plain English. Then find me every single thing in this code that puts points back on.
The follow-up that made it a story
How can someone who has been quiet for a month get their score back up?
05
Super Speed
Artifact: brief.md + prototype.html

The turning point: my first fix asked the responder to do something. I pushed back, and the direction became "the system fixes it": restore the 90s wait, stop counting misses against rank, let scores recover, and alert PM and engineering automatically when a responder's pings halve.

The prompt that set the design principle
Find a solution that doesn't put the onus on the responder

Keeping it from happening again

The review-checklist skill checks every brief the same four ways.

4.2 shipped two changes together, with no written reason for 60s, no owner after Priya left, and a Q3 commitment conversation that never happened. Each of those is a gap the checklist catches before a brief goes further.

1 · Owner

Names who owns it, in one clear place. (Every Q3 roadmap item listed Priya; the Phone App brief named Sofia.)

2 · Success measure

Metric, baseline and target. (Bulk Callout never set a baseline.)

3 · Scope consistency

Scope at the end matches the start. (Approval Chains crept into a dashboard and a spreadsheet replacement.)

4 · Problem before fix

Evidence first, then the proposal. (The 4.2 changes had no recorded problem statement.)
